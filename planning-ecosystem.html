FLAGSHIP CASE STUDY / SANITIZED

Intelligent Enterprise Planning & Execution.

From business strategy to engineering commitments, delivery and continuous improvement.

Business context

A large, cross-functional technology organization had an established planning lifecycle, but lengthy planning cycles, repetitive project assessments and limited resource visibility made reliable commitments difficult. As I worked through the lifecycle, I progressively identified and addressed gaps rather than replacing the entire process.

Transformation lifecycle

  1. Strategy alignment: Partner with Strategy and Product to translate annual and quarterly goals into projects with defined outcomes, scope and priorities.
  2. Process redesign: Reuse the standard intake mechanism, simplify requirements and establish readiness gates. Program owners validate intake quality; incomplete requests return for clarification.
  3. Intelligent demand assessment: AI-assisted review of requirements, dependencies, engineering impact, preliminary estimates, assumptions, guiding questions and confidence. Engineering retains final estimation authority.
  4. Capacity baseline: Standardize effective resource capacity using availability, leave, regional calendars, role and allocation. Team owners validate inputs; rollups support portfolio visibility.
  5. Investment decisions: Normalize demand and supply into comparable effort units, identify overbooked teams and guide leadership tradeoffs using company priorities and ranked demand.
  6. Execution readiness: Capture readiness dates and translate commitments into milestones, dependencies, owners and team-level work items.
  7. Execution capacity: Combine validated resource allocations with current sprint assignments through automated refreshes to assess ability to accommodate new demand.
  8. Feedback: Use historical execution evidence to refine future AI-assisted estimates.

Conceptual workflow: these complementary capabilities were introduced incrementally, not as a single integrated software platform.

Solutions and personal contribution

Planning transformation

Personally designed the future-state process and partnered with stakeholders on implementation, quality gates and accountability. Supporting case study.

AI Project Triage Assistant

Personally designed and developed an AI-assisted workflow combining structured intake, historical context, deterministic filtering and structured outputs. Refined it through user feedback and human review; historical execution evidence informed estimation improvements. Supporting case study.

Planned Capacity Tracker

Identified limitations in centralized capacity calculations, defined requirements and standardized the business rules for a domain-level tracker. Implementation was collaborative rather than solely my own. Team owners validated resource inputs and capacity rollups.

Portfolio utilization and decisions

Helped standardize effort units and establish views comparing demand with available capacity. Facilitated leadership decisions, highlighted gaps and risks, and presented evidence-based recommendations for prioritization and commitments.

Engineering Capacity Planner

Personally designed and developed an execution-level tool in response to leadership requests for faster assessment of unplanned demand. Automated refreshes from resource-planning and work-management systems combined allocations and assigned sprint workload in comparable units. Supporting case study · Illustrative interactive demo.

Employee experience and adoption

Product managers received actionable clarification questions, program managers gained structured intake and portfolio visibility, engineering owners received preliminary assessments and capacity views, and leadership gained evidence for investment and execution decisions. Incremental delivery addressed previously unmet needs and reused familiar workflows. The AI assistant was demonstrated to users and refined using their feedback.

Business outcomes

Observed improvements included a shorter planning cycle, less manual triage effort, higher-confidence commitments, clearer execution plans and capacity visibility, and fewer change requests. Design principles

Choose technology based on the problem: process redesign for governance gaps, deterministic rules for capacity calculations, AI for contextual assessments, and integration for current execution visibility. Keep assumptions explicit, retain human accountability and use feedback to improve future decisions.
